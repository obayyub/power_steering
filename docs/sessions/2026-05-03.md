## Robustness audit

After noticing that the same vector was picked at +scale for some test
evals and −scale for others (suspicious for a "real direction"), we
ran three CPU-only checks on every cell:

`scripts/check_signal_robustness.py`:
1. **Baseline regression ceiling** — `50 - baseline_aligned_pct`. If a
   vector at high |scale| degenerates the model toward 50/50 random
   output, that produces a known alignment shift purely from regression
   to the mean. Cells whose observed shift is in the same direction as
   this ceiling AND smaller in magnitude are noise-explainable.
2. **Monotonicity** — Spearman ρ between signed scale and alignment
   shift across the 11 scales. Real steering produces |ρ| ≈ 1; |ρ| <
   0.5 = suspicious.
3. **Logit collapse** — std of `matching_logit_diff` at chosen-best
   scale vs at scale 0. Ratio < 0.5 = the response distribution
   narrowed dramatically (degeneracy signature).

## Cosine similarity surprise

`scripts/cosine_specialists.py` →
`analysis/.../cosine_specialists.png`.

For each method, the 7 home-eval specialists (one per training eval)
live in the same R^5120 space (within-method, since each method hooks
the same layer/site across runs). Cosine matrix tests whether they
point in the same direction.

Mean off-diagonal |cos|:
- CAA: 0.35 (~25× above random ≈ 0.014 for hidden_dim 5120)
- MELBO: 0.14 (~10×)
- PI: 0.14 (~10×)

**No method has mean |cos| > 0.4.** All three produce non-random
directional overlap (so there IS some shared subspace) but **none
converge to a single canonical axis** across training prompts.

This corrects yesterday's "PI_v0 is the model's alignment axis"
overclaim. PI computes the Jacobian wrt a specific prompt's
activations — different prompts → different Jacobians → different top
singular vectors. The fact that the 7 different `pi_v0`s all win as
best generalist means they're *functionally* aligned-effective, not
*directionally* identical.

The actual headline: **alignment is encoded redundantly enough that
multiple training prompts find functionally equivalent but
directionally distinct vectors**. Consistent with alignment being a
manifold or low-rank subspace rather than a single direction. CAA's
higher mean |cos| (0.35) suggests its supervised contrast keeps it
more directionally tethered, though still not converged.

## Updated narrative for the comparison

Previous draft headline: "MELBO and PI generalize ~2× better than CAA;
PI_v0 is a real alignment axis."

Corrected:
1. **MELBO and PI's generalists transfer ~2× better than CAA's
   single-vector** (off-diagonal mean shift), but this comparison
   gives them 12 candidate vectors vs CAA's 1.
2. **CAA dominates per-eval specialist potential** for behaviors
   close to its supervised target (corrigibility, myopic-reward),
   sometimes substantially.
3. **About half of "transfer" cells in the matrix are noise-
   explainable** (regression to the mean, monotonicity failures, or
   logit collapse). The robust cells are the diagonal + the
   self-aware/power/wealth cluster's cross-transfer + a few others.
4. **No method discovers a single canonical alignment axis** in vector
   space — the discovered directions overlap above random (10-25×)
   but don't converge. This is a meaningful negative finding about
   single-axis interpretability.
